Long-distance date guide

A day to share, reflect, make memories and feel close even when you're far apart.

Before you start

  • Agree on a start time and get your phone, charger and a stable connection ready.
  • Keep water, headphones and everything you'll need for the activities within reach.
  • The day doesn't have to go perfectly: the idea is to enjoy it together and adapt the plan to your own pace.

1. Breakfast together on video call

Each of you makes your favorite breakfast and you sit down to eat together while chatting calmly.

Tip: present your breakfast as if it were a little date at a café: a pretty napkin, your favorite mug and soft music.

2. Get ready together

Keep the call on while you take turns showering, choose your clothes, do your hair and get ready for the day. Respect each other's privacy and only do on camera what feels comfortable.

Tip: choose one detail in common, like a color, an accessory or a perfume.

3. Yom Kippur: reflection and letter

Follow whatever plan you have for Yom Kippur. Set aside a quiet moment to write a letter about your relationship and to reflect individually. Afterwards, if you feel like it, share some of your answers.

A letter about us

  • What do I like most about you?
  • What is my favorite memory with you?
  • What would I like to experience with you this year?

Personal Yom Kippur reflection

The past

  • How has my connection with spirituality, faith or my deepest principles been this year?
  • Whom did I hurt this year (with words, actions or silence), and to whom do I owe a sincere apology?
  • Which moments of this past year make me proud, and which make me ashamed?
  • If the year that just ended were a book, what would the titles of its hardest chapters be, and what did they teach me?

The present

  • What is my current relationship with the transcendent, with my spirituality and with my core values?
  • What legacy or kind of energy do I bring to the world in my day-to-day life?
  • If today my fate is sealed in the Book of Life, am I living a life that is truly worthwhile, or am I living on autopilot?
  • Which toxic habits, attitudes or thoughts do I need to free myself from today?

The future

  • What are my most meaningful spiritual projects and goals for this new year?
  • How can I contribute more actively to the people and the world around me?
  • What promise do I make to myself today for the coming year?
  • What word or phrase will guide my actions so that I live a year that is more awake, more aware and full of meaning?

To close: write your guiding word or phrase beautifully on a piece of card and leave it somewhere visible as a reminder for the new year.

4. Choose an adventure or activity

Choose just one option, based on your tastes, the day's energy and the weather.

Option A: cultural visit

Each of you visits a cultural spot in your own city (a museum, exhibition, gallery or monument). Share photos, fun facts and whatever catches your eye along the way.

Option B: dance challenge

Pick the same choreography, practice it together over video call and record the result. It doesn't need to be perfect: the fun is learning it at the same time.

Option C: swimming

Go to an indoor pool and swim, each on your own. Afterwards, call each other to tell how it went. Don't take your phone into the water.

Option D: favorite outdoor spot

Each of you goes to your favorite place, ideally without revealing the destination beforehand. Buy something tasty on the way and enjoy it together over video call from your own spots.

Option E: outdoor sport

Do a shared activity that suits the weather: yoga, stretching, walking or a gentle routine. If you choose archery, only do it at a safe, supervised facility.

Weather plan: if the weather is nice, you can choose an outdoor activity; if it's raining or cold, go for the indoor cultural visit, dancing at home or swimming in an indoor pool.

5. A shared photo

After the activity, take a creative photo: each of you sets your phone on a timer and stands at one edge of the frame, leaving space towards the center. Then combine the two photos so it looks like you're touching or standing together.

Tip: try to match the camera height, pose and lighting so the montage looks more natural.

6. Head home and do the shopping

Agree on a recipe for a quick dinner. Make the ingredient list and also buy the painting supplies: two canvases, paints, brushes and palettes. If you're missing them, add cups for water and paper to protect the table.

Tip: choose an easy recipe, with ingredients you can find without hassle and that you can both make.

7. Cook and have dinner together

Make the same recipe at the same time over video call. Show each other the steps, compare how it's turning out and sit down to eat together when it's ready.

Tip: put your phone somewhere stable and safe, away from the stove, water and cutting areas.

8. Paint each other

With the canvases and paints ready, paint a portrait of each other. You can try to be realistic or turn it into a funny drawing. When you're done, show your works on camera and keep them as a memento.

Tip: don't aim for perfection; put a song on in the background and enjoy each other's reactions.

9. Movie and end of the day

Choose a movie, sync it to start at the same time and get popcorn and your favorite drinks ready. When it's over, tell each other which moment of the day you liked most and what you'd like to repeat on another date.

Tip: count down to press play at the same time, and avoid commenting on big scenes before you've both seen them.

What matters isn't following the plan to the letter, but creating a day that feels like yours.
